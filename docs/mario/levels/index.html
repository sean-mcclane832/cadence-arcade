MARIO, LEVEL BY LEVEL

One Cadence brain (cadence-net 0.91.1) plays the levels in order, learning the whole time. Two runs: a nearly blind brain (before), then a fresh brain with better senses (after). Updated 2026-10-29 16:45.

What's happening

  • One Cadence brain plays Super Mario Bros. in order, 1-1, 1-2, 1-3, ..., learning the whole time and never reset between levels. First sight (3 solo attempts before any practice) is the test: if it is learning to play Mario rather than memorizing levels, first-sight distance should rise and beat simple baselines.
  • Before: the blind brain saw the screen as 26 x 32 grey blocks, 2 frames, nothing else. Its first sight beat the best baseline on no new level; an untrained brain or random buttons did as well or better. After practice it went further on most levels (1-3 to 746, 2-2 to 1,496) but cleared none, and it forgot old levels quickly.
  • After: a fresh brain with better senses. Eyes: 4 x 4 pixel blocks (4x the detail), plus how the last 3 frames changed, each frame normalized to its own background and contrast so overground, underground and castles look alike. Body sense: Mario's own speed, rising or falling, on the ground or not, facing, size, hurt and star timers, time left.
  • Boundary: the body sense comes from the game's memory, but only about Mario himself. No enemy, block or pit positions are read; the world comes from the eyes.
  • Practice on a level: it plays on its own and learns from the reward (going right, not dying), copies its own good stretches, and gets a few coached tries where a look-ahead planner writes down what it would have pressed. The planner never takes the controls. About a tenth of its play is an earlier level again. It moves on after clearing a level solo (flag in 2 of 3) or about 38 minutes.
